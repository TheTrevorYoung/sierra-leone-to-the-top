Credits & media policy

Use great media. Credit it properly.

This page records brand and third-party media attribution used by Sierra Leone To The Top.

Current build

Official brand asset

The Sierra Leone To The Top logo used throughout this site is the approved official brand logo supplied by the project.

Photography policy

We prefer original Sierra Leone To The Top photography, contributor photography used with permission, public-domain material, or clearly licensed Creative Commons media with the required attribution.

We do not treat an image being publicly visible online as permission to reuse it.

Original photography

The Lumley Beach shoreline image used across the Home, Discover and Watch launch-stage visual system comes from a Sierra Leone To The Top-controlled field archive. Web-ready derivatives are stored with the site.

Historical archive references

The 2020 Conversations index is based on titles and dates preserved by the existing Sierra Leone To The Top YouTube channel. Guest biographies and additional claims are intentionally excluded until separately verified.

Kelvin Doe profile sources

The Kelvin Doe profile uses Sierra Leone's Ministry of Communication, Technology and Innovation for the January 2026 robotics tournament, a July 2026 long-form profile for current context, and contemporaneous 2012 reporting for his early maker story. Claims are attributed rather than presented as independent audit findings.

Diaspora & heritage sources

The Bunce Island/Gullah Geechee feature uses Sierra Leone Heritage for the site’s protection and homecoming history, the U.S. National Park Service for Gullah Geechee cultural context, UNESCO’s Bunce Island tentative-list entry for the Sierra Leone/U.S. heritage connection, and Smithsonian material for the history of Krio/Gullah linguistic scholarship.

Culture explainer sources

The Krio explainer draws on Sierra Leone’s Ministry of Tourism and Cultural Affairs, Sierra Leone Heritage, the Permanent Mission of Sierra Leone, Smithsonian collections and academic work on Krio and national identity. It is presented as an introductory synthesis rather than a definitive linguistic history.

Next Generation case-study source

The Learn2Earn analysis attributes participant and program facts to the Ministry of Communication, Technology and Innovation’s July 14, 2026 profile of James Emmanuel Paul Yambasu. The four lessons presented on Sierra Leone To The Top are our analysis of the reported sequence rather than quotations or independent verification of the employment relationship.

Business Radar sources

The READY Salone startup briefing summarizes the Sierra Leone Ministry of Communication, Technology and Innovation’s August 3, 2026 public announcement. Additional company self-descriptions may be consulted during follow-up reporting, but the launch article intentionally distinguishes ministry-reported information from independent verification.
