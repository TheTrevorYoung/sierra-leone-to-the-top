Next Generation · Case study

Training was only the first step.

A July 2026 Learn2Earn story published by Sierra Leone’s technology ministry points to something useful: the employment outcome came from combining skills with a portfolio, professional visibility and follow-through.

Source-based analysis · September 2026
The source

MoCTI profiled James Emmanuel Paul Yambasu, then a second-year Mathematics and Statistics student at Fourah Bay College, after his Learn2Earn Cohort 2 training.

Read the MoCTI profile ↗

According to the Ministry’s profile, the training program helped participants build practical digital-work assets rather than collect a certificate alone. Yambasu worked on professional profiles and a portfolio, then publicly shared his work. The Ministry says that visibility contributed to a referral shortly after the cohort ended and led to a personal-assistant role supporting climate-informed disease-surveillance work.

Sierra Leone To The Top has not independently verified the employment relationship described by the Ministry. But the sequence in the public case study is still useful because it highlights behaviors that young people can apply even outside one program.

01

Skills need evidence.

Knowing how to do something and showing that you can do it are different. A portfolio turns abstract ability into something another person can evaluate.

02

Visibility is part of employability.

The case study emphasizes LinkedIn, Upwork and a portfolio website. The larger lesson is not that one platform guarantees work; it is that useful skills create more opportunity when the right people can discover them.

03

Existing knowledge can be packaged better.

Yambasu’s university background already included quantitative and research skills. The training appears to have helped convert those capabilities into a clearer professional presentation.

04

Mentors can shorten the distance to opportunity.

The Ministry’s profile says a mentor alerted him to the program. That is a small detail with a large implication: networks often matter before the application even begins.

What a useful opportunity desk should do

This is why the Sierra Leone To The Top Youth desk should not merely repost scholarship or training announcements. When possible, we should also explain what successful applicants actually did, what skills employers noticed, what evidence applicants needed and what happened after the program ended.

What do you learn?Specific skills, not vague promises.
What do you build?Portfolio, project, credential or work sample.
Who sees it?Employers, clients, mentors or collaborators.
What comes next?A pathway beyond the training itself.
Editorial note: facts about the participant and program above are attributed to the Ministry’s published profile. Our four lessons are Sierra Leone To The Top’s analysis of that reported sequence, not claims made by the participant.
