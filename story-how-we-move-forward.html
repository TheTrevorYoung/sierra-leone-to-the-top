How We Move Forward · Method

Complaints identify pain. Solutions require more work.

Ideas & Solutions will use a five-part test to move public discussion from “something is wrong” toward evidence, realistic options and action that can be measured.

Sierra Leone To The Top editorial framework
The rule

A strong solution story should make the problem clearer without pretending the answer is easier than it is.

Read our editorial standards →

Constructive does not mean positive all the time. It means the discussion has somewhere to go. A problem may be serious. A policy may be failing. An idea may be promising. The work is to separate those statements and test each one.

01
Define

What exactly is the problem?

“Education is bad” is too broad to solve. Which students? Which level? Attendance, teaching quality, materials, exams, employability, infrastructure or something else? A useful problem statement is narrow enough to investigate.

02
Evidence

What do we actually know?

Separate measurements, records, firsthand reporting and credible research from assumptions, anecdotes and political talking points. If evidence is incomplete, say that rather than filling the gap with certainty.

03
Learn

What is already being tried?

Before proposing a new project, look for existing Sierra Leonean efforts, previous programs, local expertise and relevant examples elsewhere. Reinventing something that already exists can waste money and attention.

04
Reality

What could stop the idea from working?

Cost, maintenance, electricity, staffing, procurement, regulation, geography, public behavior, politics and institutional capacity can all turn an attractive idea into a poor implementation. Constraints belong inside the solution, not in the fine print.

05
Act + measure

What is the smallest useful next step?

Define who must act, what can be tested, what success would look like and what evidence would tell us to stop, change or expand. The goal is not a perfect national master plan every time. It may be a credible pilot, a better question or a decision supported by evidence.

The five questions

ProblemWhat exactly are we trying to change?
EvidenceHow do we know the problem exists in the form described?
Existing workWho is already trying something?
ConstraintsWhat makes implementation difficult?
Next actionWhat can be tested and measured?

What this prevents

This method is designed to resist two common failures: cynicism that stops at criticism, and optimism that jumps directly to a shiny solution. Both can avoid the hard middle — evidence, tradeoffs and implementation.

Problem → Evidence → Existing work → Constraints → Action + Measurement

Where we will use it

Energy. Transportation. Waste. Education. Tourism. Agriculture. Housing. Technology. Health systems. Public services. Governance. Any subject can enter the Ideas & Solutions desk if we can make the question specific enough and treat competing evidence fairly.

Editorial note: this is a reporting framework, not a claim that every public problem can be reduced to five simple steps. Complex issues may require competing perspectives, longer evidence reviews and multiple possible actions.
