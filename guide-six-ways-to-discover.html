Discover Sierra Leone · First look

Six places. Six different sides of Sierra Leone.

A city and coast. A conservation sanctuary. A site of global history. A rainforest island. An Atlantic archipelago. A national park. Sierra Leone cannot be understood through one kind of destination.

Planning guide · Based on official and destination sources
Before you travel

Access, operating hours, permits, transport and services can change. Use this as a first-look guide, then verify current arrangements with the destination or Sierra Leone’s official tourism bodies.

National Tourist Board portal ↗

Sierra Leone’s strongest travel story is variety. You can move from Freetown and the peninsula coast into rainforest, wildlife, islands and sites that connect Sierra Leone to the wider history of the Atlantic world.

01
Capital + coast

Freetown

The National Tourist Board describes Freetown through its markets, beaches and historical landmarks. For many visitors it is the natural starting point — city life on one side, the Western Area Peninsula and Atlantic coast on the other.

Official tourism source ↗

02
Conservation

Tacugama Chimpanzee Sanctuary

Tacugama sits within the Western Area forest landscape outside Freetown. Sierra Leone’s Ministry of Tourism says the sanctuary opened in 1995; today Tacugama combines chimpanzee conservation, education, forest protection and visitor experiences.

Ministry source ↗Tacugama ↗

03
Heritage

Bunce Island

About 32 kilometers upriver from Freetown, Bunce Island preserves the remains of a British slave-trading fortress. It is a place for historical memory and reflection, especially within the wider story connecting Sierra Leone and descendants of enslaved Africans in the Americas.

Ministry source ↗Sierra Leone Heritage ↗

04
Rainforest island

Tiwai Island Wildlife Sanctuary

Tiwai lies between two arms of the Moa River and covers about 1,200 hectares. Official tourism information says it was gazetted as a wildlife sanctuary in 1987. Tiwai’s own visitor program frames ecotourism as a way to experience the rainforest while supporting conservation and surrounding communities.

Ministry source ↗Tiwai visitor information ↗

05
Island life

Banana Islands

Southwest of the Freetown Peninsula, the Banana Islands comprise Dublin, Ricketts and uninhabited Mes-Meheux. The Ministry of Tourism describes Dublin for its beaches and Ricketts for its forests, with fishing and tourism central to island life.

Ministry source ↗

06
Wild interior

Outamba-Kilimi National Park

Declared a national park in 1995, Outamba-Kilimi consists of two areas separated by a corridor and combines lowland forest, moist savannah woodland, grassland and gallery forest. The Ministry lists primates, elephants, hippopotamuses and other wildlife among the park’s fauna.

Ministry source ↗

One country, many entry points

This list is intentionally not a “top six.” Sierra Leone has more beaches, islands, forests, mountains, heritage sites, communities and experiences than any short list can represent. The purpose is to show the range — and create a structure we can expand with original reporting.

Editorial note: descriptions above are independently summarized from the linked official or destination sources. Sierra Leone To The Top has not treated promotional claims as independently verified simply because they appear on a tourism website.
