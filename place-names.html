Stories behind the map

Why is it called that?

Every place name carries memory: migration, language, landscape, leadership, work, conflict, humor, faith or family. We want to preserve those stories before they disappear.

A national map made of stories.

This feature takes inspiration from the village-name collection maintained by Peter C. Andersen's Sierra Leone Web, which invited people to submit the origins and meanings of towns and villages. SLTT will build its own reported version: local voices, source checks, photographs, audio where possible, and clear labels when accounts differ.

Starter examples

One name can open an entire story.

These short notes are paraphrased research leads from the external archive, not copied entries. Each will need deeper local reporting before becoming a full SLTT feature.

Western Area

Hastings

The archive connects the modern name to an early-19th-century visit by British colonial official Lord Hastings and to the complex settlement history of liberated African communities in the area.

How to contribute

Tell us what your place is called—and why.

A strong submission gives us enough information to start checking the story.

What we need

Name + location

Town, village, street or neighborhood; district and chiefdom where relevant; and the language the name comes from.

What it means

Translation + explanation

What does the name mean? Who named it? Is there more than one version of the story?

Who told you

Source + memory

Name the elder, family member, local historian, book, document or institution that supports the account.

What we can show

Photo + audio

A current photograph, an old family image with rights information, or a short audio explanation can make the story much stronger.

Different stories can coexist.

Place-name origins are often oral history. When credible accounts differ, SLTT will not force a false single answer. We will label the versions, identify the sources and explain what can and cannot be verified.
