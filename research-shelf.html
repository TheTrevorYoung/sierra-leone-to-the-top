Research Shelf

Follow the source trail.

A good story should leave readers with somewhere to go next. This is our growing guide to archives, maps, books, documents and reference collections about Sierra Leone.

Sources should be visible.

The Research Shelf is not a claim that every external source is complete or current. It is a transparent starting point. We identify who maintains each collection, what it is useful for, and when readers should verify against a newer or primary source.

One foundational independent resource is The Sierra Leone Web ↗, launched by Peter C. Andersen in 1996. It remains active as a reference and historical archive even though it no longer functions as a news service.

How SLTT uses a source

Link. Check. Add context.

Archive value and present-day accuracy are not the same thing.

Primary first

Go closer to the original.

If an archive points to a law, census, report, book, court decision or public record, we try to locate the original document or authoritative edition.

Date matters

Historic facts stay historic.

Old census figures, officeholders, institutional structures and contact details are labeled by date rather than presented as current.

Rights matter

Research is not republication.

We can learn from a photograph, poem or scanned book without assuming that we have permission to republish it.

Readers can verify

Show the trail.

Whenever practical, stories include links to the records, collections or institutions that support important claims.

Historical caution

The Sierra Leone Web itself notes that external references can be incomplete or out of date. SLTT treats archive material as evidence to investigate, not as automatic present-day truth.

Know a collection we should add?

Help expand the shelf.

We are looking for university archives, family collections, district histories, museums, newspapers, oral-history projects, photographs, maps and digitized documents.
